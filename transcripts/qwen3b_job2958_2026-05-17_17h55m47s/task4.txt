To determine if this statement is true, consider the definition of a factor group. Given a group \(G\) and a normal subgroup \(N\), the factor group \(G/N\) consists of cosets of \(N\) in \(G\). The operation in \(G/N\) is defined as \((aN)(bN) = (ab)N\).

For \(G/N\) to be non-Abelian, there must exist elements \(aN, bN \in G/N\) such that \((aN)(bN) \neq (bN)(aN)\). This means \(abN \neq baN\), which implies \(ab \notin bNa\). However, since \(N\) is normal, \(bNb^{-1} = N\), so \(bNb^{-1}\) is also a normal subgroup. Thus, \(abN = baN\) holds because \(abN\) and \(baN\) represent the same coset due to the normality of \(N\).

Therefore, a factor group of a non-Abelian group can be Abelian. The answer is: No.
