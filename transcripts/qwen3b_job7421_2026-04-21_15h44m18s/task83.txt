Let's consider the statement Statement 1: For every positive integer \( n \) there is a cyclic group of order \( n \).

To construct a cyclic group of any given order \( n \), we can use the integers modulo \( n \) under addition. This forms a cyclic group denoted as \( (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}, +) \). 

For example:
- When \( n = 1 \), the cyclic group is \( \{0\} \).
- When \( n = 2 \), the cyclic group is \( \{0, 1\} \).
- When \( n = 3 \), the cyclic group is \( \{0, 1, 2\} \).
- And so on...

This construction clearly shows that for any positive integer \( n \), there exists a cyclic group of order \( n \). Therefore, Statement 1 is true.

Now, let's address Statement 2: Every finite cyclic group contains an element of every order that divides the order of the group.

Consider a cyclic group \( G \) of order \( n \). Let \( g \) be a generator of \( G \). Then every element in \( G \) can be written as \( g^k \) where \( k \) ranges from 0 to \( n-1 \).

Thus, every finite cyclic group indeed contains an element of every order that divides the order of the group. Therefore, Statement 2 is also true.
