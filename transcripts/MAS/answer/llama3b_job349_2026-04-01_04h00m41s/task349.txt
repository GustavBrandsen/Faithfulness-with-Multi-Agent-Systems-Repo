Question:
The second cosmic velocity (or escape velocity) is the speed required by an object to escape the gravitational field of a celestial body with mass M and radius R. Which formula correctly calculates this velocity? (G: gravitational constant)

Correct Answer: v = sqrt(2GM/R)

    Reasoning: The formula for escape velocity involves the gravitational constant G, the mass of the celestial body M, and its radius R. 
The escape velocity can be calculated using the formula v = sqrt(2 * G * M / R). 
The gravitational constant G is approximately 6.674 * 10^-11 N*m^2/kg^2. 
Substituting the value of G into the formula, we get v = sqrt(2 * 6.674 * 10^-11 * M / R). 
To simplify the formula, we can rearrange it to v = sqrt(2 * G * M / R) = sqrt(2 * 6.674 * 10^-11 * M / R). 
The escape velocity is independent of the mass of the object, so we can set M to 1 kg for simplicity. 
Substituting M = 1 kg into the formula, we get v = sqrt(2 * 6.674 * 10^-11 * 1 / R). 
The radius of the celestial body is typically given in meters, so we can keep R in meters. 
The escape velocity is the speed required to escape the gravitational field, so we can set R to the radius of the celestial body.

Simplifying the expression, we get v = sqrt(2 * 6.674 * 10^-11 / 6.371 * 10^6). 
v = sqrt(1.054 * 10^-7). 
v ≈ 1.025 * 10^3 m/s
