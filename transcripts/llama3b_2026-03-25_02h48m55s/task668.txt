A change in blood pressure is detected by the:

  Person 1 answered: baroreceptors.
    Reasoning: The baroreceptors in the walls of blood vessels detect changes in blood pressure by sensing the stretch or compression of the vessel walls, which is caused by the pressure exerted by the blood flowing through them.
The baroreceptors are located in the carotid sinus and aortic arch, where the blood pressure is highest.
When blood pressure increases, the baroreceptors are stretched, triggering a signal that is sent to the brain, which responds by reducing heart rate and dilating blood vessels to lower blood pressure.
When blood pressure decreases, the baroreceptors are compressed, triggering a signal that is sent to the brain, which responds by increasing heart rate and constricting blood vessels to raise blood pressure.
The baroreceptors play a crucial role in maintaining homeostasis and regulating blood pressure in the body
